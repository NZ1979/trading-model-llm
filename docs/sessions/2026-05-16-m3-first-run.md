## Findings

### 1. The single LLM trade lost $19.32

MSFT on 2026-05-15 at 10:45 EDT. LLM said Buy; base said Hold (no fill).
This is the entirety of § 4 divergence. Not enough data to draw any
signal-quality conclusion from one trade.

### 2. The LLM is uniformly unconfident — biggest finding

| Confidence | Count | % |
|---|---|---|
| 0–19 | 238 | 13.7% |
| 20–29 | 1,189 | **68.3%** |
| 30–39 | 294 | 16.9% |
| 40–49 | 20 | 1.1% |
| 50+ | 1 | <0.1% |

Median ~25. Max 55. The T2 escalation band starts at 50, so only **one**
decision in the whole week even approached it. This explains the zero T2
escalations cleanly: there was nothing to escalate.

This is honest from the model's perspective (large-caps in a quiet week
with no sentiment context — there genuinely isn't a high-conviction
signal). But it also means the T1↔T2↔T3 architecture is functionally
collapsing to T1-only on this kind of input.

### 3. Setup label sprawl — design-level issue

§ 5's `setup_label` frequency table is **925 lines long** (report lines
103–1027). The LLM is generating freeform setup descriptions like
"Late-day exhaustion, 5min to close" and "EOD consolidation, insufficient
setup" rather than picking from a fixed enum. Each unique string is
counted separately, so the table is basically uncountable.

This breaks aggregation entirely. You can't bucket ~900 unique labels for
analysis or for the failure-pattern matching in § 6.

### 4. T1 schema failures: 2 of 1,742 (0.11%)

Haiku returned malformed JSON twice. Synthetic Holds with
`setup_label="schema_invalid_t1"` emitted per Rule 18 fail-loud. Provenance
column = `t1_failed`. No silent degradation. Worth a one-time grep of
`docs/sessions/2026-05-16-m3-first-run.log` to capture what Haiku actually
returned in those two cases.

### 5. § 5b regime + § 5d tier_provenance both populated correctly

## Open hypothesis: is the engine working correctly?

**Added post-commit `63a4b3c` in response to user pushback on the
"quiet week" framing.**

The initial framing of the 1-trade result was: "both engines correctly
identified that nothing was happening on these names in a quiet week."
That framing is **wrong on two counts**:

1. **The week wasn't quiet.** Per user-supplied data (cross-checked
   against the universe of large-cap earnings reports for 2026-05-11 →
   2026-05-15), the week saw 5–20% catalyst-driven moves in named
   large-caps: Cisco +7%, Applied Materials +9%, CoreWeave +18%,
   Alibaba -6%, Deere +5%, Take-Two +11%, Nu Holdings +12%,
   Constellation Energy +10%, D-Wave Quantum +20%, Hims & Hers +15%.

2. **The wrong universe was tested.** Two of those names — **HIMS
   (+15%) and QBTS (+20%)** — are on the existing
   `config/watchlist_dynamic.json` (the 500-name Russell 2000 momentum
   list main.py refreshes daily). The strategy's pre-curated watchlist
   included names that had real catalyst moves that week. The smoke
   run was pointed at AAPL/NVDA/MSFT/TSLA/AMZN instead — mega-caps with
   structurally lower gap/momentum characteristics, none of which
   reported earnings that week.

So the 1-trade result tells us the pipeline works end-to-end. It does
**not** establish whether the LLM signal generator has edge, because the
test wasn't pointed at conditions where edge would be visible.

**The standing open hypothesis (user-raised, 2026-05-16):** the LLM model
may not be working correctly. Specific symptoms that prompted the
suspicion:

- **Confidence ceiling at 55.** Across 1,742 decisions, the maximum
  confidence emitted was 55. The T2 escalation band starts at 50.
  Architecturally, T2/T3 only fire on decisions in the 50–75
  uncertainty band; if the model never emits above 55 even on names
  with real signal, the tiered architecture functionally collapses to
  T1-only on every run.

- **Setup label repetition.** Top labels are "Flat Consolidation—No
  Edge" (47), "Late-session post-gap consolidation" (25), "Post-gap
  consolidation, neutral technicals" (23), "Post-gap consolidation
  chop" (16). These read like template responses, not setup-specific
  observations. Possible mode collapse to a small set of "safe Hold"
  rationales.

- **2 schema failures in 1,742 calls (0.11%).** Low rate, but the two
  failures were never inspected — they could reveal a prompt or schema
  issue the rest of the run masked.

This hypothesis is **not yet validated or refuted**. The next M3 chat
should investigate it *before* any expensive re-run, per follow-up #7
below.

## Follow-ups for future M3 sessions

Captured here as candidates for sub-tasks; none are blocking the next M3
run.

1. **Setup label enum (or post-hoc bucketing).** Either constrain
   `setup_label` to a small enum in the prompt schema (cleanest), or have
   the report bucket-classify freeform strings into a handful of
   categories before tabulating § 5. Without one or the other, § 5 is
   unreadable on any non-trivial run.

2. **`--quiet-missing-sentiment` flag** or per-day aggregate stat. With an
   empty sentiment fixture the harness emits one WARNING per (article ×
   ticker) lookup. The 5-day × 5-ticker run produced thousands of
   identical-shape warnings, drowning out any other signal in the log.
   Real fixtures will reduce this, but the harness should aggregate
   missing-sentiment counts at end-of-day even with full fixtures, not
   per-line warn.

3. **Real sentiment fixture export.** Open a gap-and-go-anchored Cowork
   session and run the procedure in `data/replay/fixtures/README.md` §
   "Export procedure". Without sentiment data, the divergence section,
   the sentiment-informed branches of `evaluate_trade`, and any
   correlation between confidence and news context cannot be evaluated.

4. **Wider replay window or different universe.** 5 days × 5 large-caps
   generated 1 trade — not enough to evaluate signal quality. The next
   run needs either (a) a month-long window for more trade samples, (b)
   more tickers in the same large-cap universe, or (c) a deliberate
   universe-experiment swap to the existing `watchlist_dynamic.json` (500
   Russell 2000 momentum names) to see whether the LLM's edge tracks the
   base codebase's small-cap setup universe or whether it differs.

   **Framing correction (added post-commit `55bf724`):** the LLM model
   replaces the *signal engine*, not the *universe*. Per
   `docs/LLM_MODEL_CHARTER.md`, the premise is that an LLM makes better
   Buy/Sell/Hold decisions on the same candidate stream the rule-based
   gap-and-go strategy sees (same pre-filter, same watchlist style). The
   base codebase's edge lives in small-cap momentum because that's where
   gap-and-go was designed for; whether the LLM model's edge lives there
   too, or in some other slice (large-caps, regime-stratified subsets,
   different sectors), is an **open empirical question** that M3 is
   supposed to help answer. Don't pre-suppose the universe — pick the
   next window as a deliberate experiment.

5. **Confidence calibration vs. escalation floor.** If 99% of T1 decisions
   land below 50, either the prompt is producing low-confidence outputs
   even when conviction is warranted (prompt issue), or the escalation
   floor of 50 is mis-calibrated against what Haiku actually emits. Worth
   checking once a larger run produces a representative distribution.

6. **Investigate the 2 T1 schema failures.** Grep the run log for the two
   `schema_invalid_t1` lines, capture the raw Haiku response shape, and
   decide whether to (a) loosen the schema validator, (b) add a JSON
   repair step, or (c) leave as-is since 0.11% is acceptable noise.

7. **Diagnosis-first before any blind re-run.** Per the Open Hypothesis
   section above, the next M3 session should investigate whether the LLM
   engine is producing meaningful per-tick decisions or has mode-collapsed
   into template Holds. Specific diagnostic steps, in order:

   a. Grep `docs/sessions/2026-05-16-m3-first-run.log` for the two
      `schema_invalid_t1` lines + 10 lines of surrounding context.
      Captures what Haiku actually returned in the failure cases.

   b. Query `docs/reports/replay_results.db` for the MSFT Buy at
      2026-05-15T10:45:00-04:00 — pull full `reasoning`, `confidence`,
      `setup_label`, and the `LLMContext` payload that was sent in.
      The single non-Hold decision is the highest-information row in
      the entire run.

   c. Random-sample 10 Hold decisions from the DB across different
      (ticker, day, time-of-day) combinations. Read the `reasoning`
      strings side-by-side. Are they describing the actual chart context
      (varied, specific) or generic templates (boilerplate, repeated
      phrases)? Boilerplate at scale → mode collapse → engine issue.
      Varied + specific → engine is reasoning, just conservatively →
      calibration issue.

   d. Tabulate the action distribution conditional on confidence band.
      A healthy engine should show some Buys/Sells in the 30–50 band,
      not just the 50+ band. If 100% of <50-confidence decisions are
      Holds, the model has implicitly learned "Hold unless very sure" —
      which would make the T2 escalation band (50–75) the *only* place
      non-Hold actions originate, defeating the tiered design.

   e. **Only after a→d:** decide what the re-run is testing. If
      diagnosis says calibration, re-run on the small-cap watchlist
      (HIMS, QBTS, CRWV, CEG, NU and 2–3 quiet-name controls) to see
      whether confidence rises on real catalysts. If diagnosis says
      mode collapse / prompt issue, fix the prompt or schema first,
      THEN re-run.

   This step is not optional. A blind re-run on different names without
   diagnosis risks burning another 2–4 hours of compute to surface the
   same uniformly-Hold pattern, with no additional information about
   *why*.
